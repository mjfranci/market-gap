# Kepner-Tregoe Framework: Market Research Application

The Kepner-Tregoe (KT) framework structures problem-solving through four sequential questions. This workspace applies them across stages 02 and 03.

---

## KT Situational Appraisal (Stage 02: Market Research)

### What is happening

Document the observable facts about the market:
- Market definition and scope (who, what, where)
- Market size and growth trajectory (TAM, CAGR if available)
- Key players and their relative positions
- Current dominant business models
- Recent significant changes or events
- Emerging trends and early signals

### Why did it happen

Identify root causes and structural drivers:
- Technology shifts that enabled or disrupted the market
- Regulatory or macroeconomic forces
- Changes in customer behavior or expectations
- Competitive dynamics that shaped the current structure
- Historical decisions or inflection points that explain today's state

---

## KT Decision Analysis (Stage 03: Problem Identification)

### What should we do

Identify the opportunity surface:
- Whitespace: segments or needs no player adequately addresses
- Underserved segments: groups whose needs are partially met but poorly
- Over-served segments: groups paying for more than they need (disruption candidates)
- Job-to-be-done gaps: outcomes customers want but cannot reliably achieve
- Distribution or access gaps: customers who cannot reach existing solutions

State the primary problem as a job-to-be-done:
"[Customer segment] needs to [achieve outcome] but cannot [because of specific barrier]."

Select the single most compelling opportunity based on: problem acuity, market size, and solver fit.

---

## KT Potential Problem Analysis (Stage 03: Problem Identification)

### What could go wrong next

For the selected opportunity, identify:
- **Timing threats:** forces that will close the window (incumbent response, regulation, technology shift)
- **Structural barriers:** reasons this problem remains unsolved (moat of existing players, cost, distribution)
- **Assumption risks:** beliefs the opportunity framing depends on -- if wrong, the opportunity disappears
- **Adjacent threats:** problems that look like opportunities but are actually traps

For each risk, note: likelihood (low/medium/high) and impact if it materializes.

---

## Output Standards

KT outputs in this workspace (market-analysis.md and problem-brief.md) must be:
- **Grounded:** every major claim tied to an observable fact, data point, or named source
- **Specific:** no generic statements without a number or comparator
- **Actionable:** the problem statement must be specific enough that a solution can be designed against it
